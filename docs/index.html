Negative result · 2026

Giving a model a pen does not reveal how it thinks.

We built a probe for spatial reasoning out of Turtle Graphics, on the theory that imperative relative commands would force a language model to hold spatial state. We then tested the probe. It does not work, and the reason is structural — not a matter of better shapes or better conditions.

The idea, and what happened to it

Frontier models read images and emit images or SVG. SVG is declarative — a list of absolute coordinates. Turtle Graphics is imperative — a pen with a position and a heading, where each command is interpreted against accumulated state. So the argument went: force a model to reproduce an image through turtle commands rather than SVG, and it must hold spatial state. Measure how that state degrades, and you have measured something about spatial reasoning.

The instrument was built. Then the instrument was tested, before spending anything on models. Four things fell out, and the fourth one closes the line of enquiry.

The four findings

01

The items are degenerate

A 199-command spiral is reproduced by eight numbers to within a quarter of one percent of its diameter. Turn angle is near-constant and step length near-smooth, so two low-order polynomials carry the whole shape. Nothing is tracked.

02

There is a second door

An item must be expensive both to express as a small program and to express as a few absolute moves. We only guarded the first. A regular zigzag is drawn by one absolute move. Six of nine families are dead.

03

Degeneracy is not intrinsic

It is a property of shape × grading tolerance × search class — all three chosen by the experimenter. The same zigzag needs 199 moves at 1% tolerance and 1 move at 2%. The threshold is the finding.

04

It cannot be tested

Every quantity the task needs is either readable locally off the image or recomputable by summing the model's own emitted tokens. No item design forces the computation into a non-textual channel. The question is unreachable.

Why the route cannot work

To emit the command for segment k, a model needs the turn from segment k−1 to segment k. That is the angle between two adjacent pieces of the picture — a local quantity it can read straight off the image. It does not need to know its own heading, because the turtle maintains that.

So a misread bend does not corrupt the model's estimate of anything. It corrupts the turtle's position, downstream, because the turtle integrated the error. The accumulation happens in the turtle, not in the model — which means the drift metric measures per-step angular precision, a perceptual acuity measure, and not state tracking.

The structural problem

There is no configuration of this task that forces the computation into a non-textual channel. Every quantity it requires is either readable locally from the image or recomputable by summing the model's own emitted tokens. That is a property of an architecture whose only output is text, and no amount of item design changes it.

What survives is narrower and still worth asking: do models have a perceptual shortcut that beats a text-strategy baseline? That is measurable and falsifiable. It is not AGI, and the gap between those two framings is the whole distance between this being a paper and being a press release.
