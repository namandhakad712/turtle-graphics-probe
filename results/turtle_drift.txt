ERROR PROPAGATION: turtle vs absolute-coordinate formats
==========================================================================

RMS endpoint displacement, as a fraction of the drawing's diameter.
Heading noise per turn, 4000 trials per cell.

 segments | sigma= 0.5 deg | sigma= 1.0 deg | sigma= 2.0 deg
------------------------------------------------------------
       50 |          4.4% |          8.8% |         17.4%
      100 |          6.1% |         12.2% |         24.3%
      200 |          8.8% |         17.5% |         34.5%
      500 |         13.8% |         27.4% |         53.2%
     1000 |         19.3% |         38.0% |         71.7%

      SVG | flat, indep. of N | flat, indep. of N | flat, indep. of N

Turtle drift grows with segment count. SVG drift does not.

==========================================================================
OUTPUT COST: the same circle
==========================================================================

------------------------------------------------
       50 |           300 |         20 |     15x
      100 |           600 |         20 |     30x
      200 |         1,200 |         20 |     60x
      500 |         3,000 |         20 |    150x
     1000 |         6,000 |         20 |    300x

A circle is one SVG element and N turtle segments.
The turtle form costs orders of magnitude more tokens to say less.

==========================================================================
WHAT THIS IMPLIES
==========================================================================

1. Turtle adds a failure mode that is not perceptual. A model can
   see the image perfectly and still fail, purely by drifting.
2. That failure grows with path length, so harder drawings fail for
   reasons unrelated to task difficulty -- an unfair, non-monotonic
   difficulty curve.
3. Grading cannot separate 'could not see it' from 'lost track of
   heading'. Two very different hypotheses, one score.

This does not make turtle worthless. It makes it a probe for
sequential state tracking, not for visual reasoning.
